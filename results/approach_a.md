# Approach A and the baseline

Generated by `HEADLESS=1 python trials.py`, which runs 3700 trials in about 66 minutes on 7 workers. This is step 9 of the
spec's section 16. The table at the end is its output; the text here is the interpretation. Every row is position-only with
30 ms of latency and 100 trials, and trial n is the same toss and sensor noise in every row. Every catch motion is planned at
0.85 of rated speed. Approach B hasn't been run.

## Why catch motions are planned at 0.85 of rated speed

An earlier version of this grid planned catch motions at the toss's 0.986 of rated speed. There 0.15 s looked like the best
window, and this report recommended 3 mm, 0.04 m/s, 0.15 s, at 68 of 100 against the baseline's 44. **That result didn't hold
up.** Drake doesn't enforce joint speed limits, and 61 of those 68 catches went more than 5% over rated speed. These are the
same tosses at σ_p = 3 mm, σ lateral = 0.04 m/s, with only the plan's limit changed:

- **The spec's expectation of failures at 0.15 s was right** once speed is held near the rating. The shortest motion to the
  nominal catch grows from 0.400 s to 0.436 s, so the latest start falls from 0.175 s to 0.140 s.
- **0.85 is a judgement.** It is the highest level tried where no trial at 0.125 s or 0.15 s goes past 1.05x, and where a noiseless
  catch peaks at 0.96 to 0.99.
- **The limit is on the plan, not the arm.** At 0.10 s a few trials still go well past it. In the recommended cell's six fastest
  trials, the peak is joint 6 about 0.16 s into the motion, where the plan has that joint at its limit. It isn't left over from the
  brake: joint 6 starts the motion at 0.15 of rated speed. Nothing in the simulation stops that overshoot.
- **The baseline barely notices.** It goes from 81 to 80 at 0.02 m/s and doesn't change at 0.04 or 0.06 m/s.

Why that one:

- **Each approach has room to show.** The baseline catches 44, Approach A 68, and a perfect sensor 91. A sits inside the 15% to 85%
  that section 15 asks for, with the baseline well below it and room above it for B. At 0.02 m/s the baseline already catches 80,
  because that much toss noise fits inside the hand's tolerance.
- **The window stays where the spec put it,** so B's first solve doesn't have to move.
- **Most of A's misses are the kind B is meant to fix.** Of its 32 misses, 22 had a motion but put the hand in the wrong place or closed
  it at the wrong time; a perfect sensor on the same tosses has no such misses. Only 10 had no motion. At 0.125 s it's the other way
  round, 38 and 6.
- **2 mm is already one of section 6.3's levels,** so the sweep stays at 4500 trials.
- **It holds up on tosses the grid never used.** Trials 100 to 199, which aren't in the CSV, give A 70 (60-78) against the baseline's 32
  (24-42), with 44 only A and 6 only the baseline, p = 3e-8. A's 68 wasn't flattered by picking the cell from these tosses; if anything
  the baseline's 44 was high.

What it costs:

- **Two numbers move from the spec's cell**, σ_p from 5 mm to 2 mm and σ lateral from 0.02 to 0.04 m/s, and B's hypothesis is written at
  the old one. Picking the cell after seeing these results has to be stated in the paper. B hasn't been run, so it wasn't tuned to B's result.
- **2 mm is a good sensor,** and it is the only nonzero σ_p tried where one look clearly beats none. At 0.10 s, 3 mm is level with the
  baseline at best, and 5 mm is below it at every toss noise. The paper should say so.
- **Some catches rely on overspeed:** 3 of the 68 go past 1.05x, and 9 of the 70 on the fresh tosses, about 9% over both. The worst
  trial in the grid's cell reaches 1.10x. At 0.125 s none go past it on either set.
- **On-pad catches are few:** 13 here and 14 on the fresh tosses. 0.125 s gets 17 and 10, so at this cell neither window is ahead on that
  measure.
- **A gets no charge for solve time**, which has a median of 0.37 s and a p95 of 0.72 s in this cell.

The main alternative is 2 mm, 0.06, 0.10 s, the widest gap over the baseline, 58 to 21, and 55 to 15 on the fresh tosses. But 23 of its
tosses have no motion, the perfect sensor falls to 79, and at a baseline of 21 there's little left to compare against. If the team would
rather not count any catch past 1.05x, 2 mm, 0.04, 0.125 s is the other choice, 56 and 54 on the fresh tosses, at the price of 38 tosses
with no motion and little left for B to fix.

## Findings

1. **A6 passes.** With a perfect sensor and no toss noise, Approach A catches 100 of 100 with 100% contact at 0.10 s and at 0.125 s,
   against the goal of 50% and 90%. So does the baseline. None of those catches is on the pad: the noiseless catch is a perch of about
   1.5 cm (`results/catch_timing.md`, finding 4). At 0.15 s it has no motion at all.
2. **At the spec's nominal cell, not looking beats Approach A, 80 to 16.** Section 14 predicted A well below a coin flip there, and it
   is, 1 point above the 15% that section 15 says should move the cell. Lowering σ_p to 3 mm, as section 14 suggests, gives 43, which is
   near a coin flip but still far below the baseline. The fit's own error is larger than the toss noise it is there to correct.
3. **0.15 s doesn't work.** On the same tosses it loses to 0.10 s in all six cells compared (p < 0.001 in each, with no correction for
   making six comparisons). A perfect sensor catches 4, 12 and 17 of 100 as toss noise rises, and no toss at all without toss noise.
4. **Between 0.10 s and 0.125 s, the better window depends on the sensor.** At 2 mm 0.10 s is ahead, by 23 to 11 at 0.04 m/s (p = 0.06)
   and 23 to 8 at 0.06 m/s (p = 0.01). At 3 mm they're level. At 5 mm 0.125 s is ahead, by 16 to 7 (p = 0.09) and 13 to 4 (p = 0.05).
   At 0.02 m/s neither is clearly ahead at any of those three, and with a perfect sensor 0.10 s is always ahead. That's 12 comparisons
   with no correction. What waiting buys is a better fit, and what it costs is reach: at 2 mm and 0.04 m/s, no motion goes from 10 to 38
   and misses with a motion fall from 22 to 6.
5. **Approach A beats the baseline only when its sensor is good enough for the toss noise.** At 0.10 s and 2 mm it is level at 0.02 m/s
   (72 to 80, p = 0.2) and ahead at 0.04 and 0.06 m/s. At 3 mm it is behind at 0.02 m/s and within noise above that. At 5 mm it is behind
   everywhere. The baseline falls from 80 to 44 to 21 as the toss gets noisier.
6. **The speed limit costs more as toss noise grows.** At 0.10 s and 0.02 m/s it changes little: 72 either way at 2 mm,
   16 either way at 5 mm. At 2 mm it takes 0.04 m/s from 70 to 68 and 0.06 m/s from 67 to 58. A perfect sensor goes from 100 to 91 at
   0.04 m/s and from 92 to 79 at 0.06 m/s, with 9 and 21 tosses out of reach.
7. **On-pad catches are a minority everywhere except with a perfect sensor,** which gets 44 to 58 at 0.10 s. They don't rank the windows
   the way the headline does: with a noisy sensor 0.125 s has more on-pad catches than 0.10 s in every cell, even at 2 mm, where it has
   fewer catches at 0.04 and 0.06 m/s. On the fresh tosses at 2 mm and 0.04 m/s that reverses, 14 to 10, so it isn't a stable difference
   there. This uses the stricter definition, within 1 cm along both axes.
8. **Re-runs repeat.** Six cells run twice, 600 trials at the speed limit, changed no outcome, and every count matched. Measurements
   differ by up to 1.9 mm, and a dropped ball's resting place by up to 12.6 cm. Details are in `planning/assumptions.md`.

## Time per trial and the Week 4 budget

A trial takes 7.4 s on average on one of 7 workers (median 7.4 s, p95 9.8 s). Solves that find a motion have a median of 0.32 s
across Approach A's rows, and in the recommended cell a median of 0.37 s, a p95 of 0.72 s, and at most 1.10 s. Section 13.4's 4500 trials
are 1500 per approach. The baseline and A would take about 55 minutes. B replans up to about 19 times per trial, so if warm starts
didn't help at all, its 1500 trials would take about another 55 minutes. That is about 2 hours at worst, which fits Week 4 without
cutting cells. B's solve times have yet to be measured.
