Why that one:

- **Each approach has room to show.** Approach A sits above the baseline, inside the 15% to 85% that section 15 asks for, and
  a perfect sensor catches 100 at this toss noise (with a 0.10 s window). So the baseline, A and B can each land somewhere
  different. At 0.02 m/s the baseline already catches 81, because that much toss noise fits inside the hand's tolerance.
- **It gives Approach A its better window.** Comparing at 0.10 s would flatter B, since part of B's gain would be what A could
  have had by waiting.
- **Its misses are the trade-off B is meant to escape.** Waiting gets a better fit but leaves less time to reach the ball:
  from 0.10 s to 0.15 s, no motion rises from 5 to 21 and misses with a motion fall from 55 to 11. B could start at 0.10 s
  and refine, if its first solve doesn't move with A's window.

What it costs:

- **Three numbers move from the spec's cell**, and B's hypothesis is written at the old one. Picking the cell after seeing
  these results has to be stated in the paper. B hasn't been run, so it wasn't tuned to B's result.
- **Most of A's misses here are for lack of time, not a bad fit**, so if B has to start at 0.15 s as well, B has little to gain.
- **A gets no charge for solve time**, and 0.15 s leaves only 15 ms of margin against the arm's 0.41 s minimum motion.
- **68 is a little flattering** as an estimate of A, because the cell was picked from these same tosses. Trials past number 99
  would give a clean figure.

The main alternative is 5 mm, 0.02, 0.15 s. It moves only the window and is the nearest to the spec's coin flip, and its misses
are mostly a bad fit, which B addresses directly. But the baseline beats it, 34 to 8, so the paper would be saying that one look
is worse than none, and B would have to beat 81 for sensing to pay off at all.

## Findings

1. **A6 passes.** With a perfect sensor and no toss noise, Approach A catches 100 of 100 with 100% contact, against the goal of
   50% and 90%. So does the baseline. None of those catches is on the pad: the noiseless catch is a 1.45 cm perch
   (`results/catch_timing.md`, finding 4).
2. **At the spec's nominal cell, not looking beats Approach A, 81 to 16.** Section 14 predicted A well below a coin flip there,
   and it is, 1 point above the 15% that section 15 says should move the cell. Lowering sigma_p to 3 mm, as section 14 suggests,
   gives 41, which is near a coin flip but still far below the baseline. The fit's own error is larger than the toss noise it is
   there to correct.
3. **0.15 s beats 0.10 s, where the spec expected failures.** It catches more on the same tosses in 8 of the 9 cells compared
   (p < 0.05 in each, with no correction for making 9 comparisons), and ties the other, 65 to 67 at 2 mm and 0.06 m/s. At
   0.02 m/s it goes from 16 to 55 at 5 mm, from 41 to 82 at 3 mm, and from 72 to 97 at 2 mm.
4. **What the window costs is reach, and it grows with toss noise.** At 3 mm, no motion goes from 0 to 5 at 0.02 m/s, from 5 to 21
   at 0.04 m/s, and from 10 to 34 at 0.06 m/s. With a motion the hand nearly always touches the ball, so contact is close to 100
   minus no motion in every 0.15 s row. Up to 7 tosses at 7 mm are rejected and fall back to the baseline's motion, and at most 1
   at 3 mm or less.
5. **Approach A beats the baseline only when its sensor is good enough for the toss noise.** At 0.15 s and 0.02 m/s it needs better
   than 3 mm: 97 at 2 mm, level at 3 mm. At 0.04 m/s, better than 5 mm: 68 at 3 mm, level at 5 mm. At 0.06 m/s it is ahead at 2 and
   3 mm and within noise at 5 and 7 mm. The baseline falls from 81 to 44 to 21 as the toss gets noisier.
6. **On-pad catches are a minority everywhere except with a perfect sensor,** which gets 52 to 54. They rank the cells about the
   same way the headline does. This uses the stricter definition, within 1 cm along both axes, so on-pad counts are lower than
   in earlier drafts of the timing report, which checked only along the fingers.
7. **Re-runs repeat.** Six cells run twice, 600 trials, changed no outcome, and every count matched. Measurements differ by up to
   0.21 mm, and a dropped ball's resting place by up to 1.24 cm. Details are in `planning/assumptions.md`.

## Time per trial and the Week 4 budget

A trial takes 7.5 s on average on one of 7 workers (median 7.4 s, p95 9.8 s). Of that, the solve has a median of 0.42 s and a
p95 of 0.64 s, at most 1.15 s. Section 13.4's 4500 trials are 1500 per approach. The baseline and A would take about 54 minutes.
B replans up to about 19 times per trial, so if warm starts didn't help at all, its 1500 trials would take about another 55
minutes. That is about 2 hours at worst, which fits Week 4 without cutting cells. B's solve times have yet to be measured.
