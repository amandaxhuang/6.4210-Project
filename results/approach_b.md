# Approach B against Approach A and the baseline

Generated by `HEADLESS=1 python replan_trials.py`, which runs 3200 trials in about 66 minutes on 7 workers (they were last run in two batches). This is B7 and steps 7 to 9 of
the plan in `planning/approach-b-closed-loop-replanning.md`. The tables at the end are its output; the text here is the interpretation.
Every row is position-only with 30 ms of latency and 100 trials, and trial n is the same toss and sensor noise in every row. Every catch
motion is planned at 0.85 of rated speed.

Unless a row says otherwise, Approach B means this: a first solve that takes no time and is identical to Approach A's, then one solver
at a time, with warm-started 12-knot replans that take over 45 ms after their data, up to 0.47 s after release. That is replanning at about 22 Hz.
`results/replan_timing.md` set those numbers before any B catch result existed. The nominal cell was chosen from A's and the baseline's
results before B ran, so it wasn't tuned to B.

**The hypothesis holds at the nominal cell, and its predicted shape holds where it was tested.** The gap to A is 0, 22 and 56 tosses at
σ_p = 0, 2 and 5 mm. This doesn't test 10 or 20 mm, or the predicted collapse at high toss noise: those belong to the Week 4 sweep.

## Findings

1. **B beat A and lost nothing to it.** No toss in any cell was caught only by A at the chosen settings. B caught all 22 and all 21 of the
   nominal cell's tosses where A had a motion and missed. It also catches more cleanly: 48 against 13 on the pad, and 43 against 14 on the
   fresh tosses. Counting only catches within 1.05x of rated speed it is 86 against 65, and 82 against 61.
2. **The control holds.** With a perfect sensor, A and B both catch 91 of the same tosses, with no disagreement on any toss. So B isn't
   winning for some reason other than data, which section 12 of the spec gives as the sign that something isn't shared. With replanning
   turned off, B's trial records match A's (B3, in the tests).
3. **The mechanism is the one the spec predicts.** From A to B at 2 mm, the median predicted miss falls from 1.5 to 0.2 cm and the timing
   error from 4.3 to 1.8 ms. B moved its target by a median of 1.4 cm and at most 3.8 cm after the first solve, and its close decision by a
   median of 2 ms and at most 12 ms (18 ms on the fresh tosses). A's catches fall off as that movement grows, 22, 19, 19 and then 8 of about 22, while
   B's stay at all of them. At 5 mm A's are 4, 5, 2 and 0 of about 18.
4. **At 2 mm B is at a ceiling, so the settings can't be told apart there.** Every setting tried catches exactly 90, because each catches
   every toss it has a motion for. So the checks were repeated at σ_p = 5 mm, where B misses tosses it has a motion for. **5 mm is the more
   informative cell for comparing versions of B**, and the Week 4 sweep's nominal cell has little room above it.
5. **B's advantage isn't an artifact of free compute.** At 5 mm: no delay catches 74, 45 ms catches 67, and 90 ms catches 61, against A's 11.
   Five of the no-delay row's 7 extra catches come from retrying a failed first solve, which a delay leaves no time for. At 2 mm all three catch 90.
6. **One more look gets most of it. The paper should say so plainly.** Replanning every 180 ms leaves room for just one replan, taking over at
   0.326 s, and that catches 90 at 2 mm, as many as anything, and 59 at 5 mm. Replanning more often gives 64 at every 90 ms, 67 at
   every 45 ms, and 74 at every 20 ms with no delay. So at this toss, what these results support is looking again, more than looking
   continually. At 2 mm replanning repeatedly adds nothing to a single replan. At 5 mm a single replan gains 48 tosses over A, and
   replanning every 45 ms gains 8 more.
7. **Replanning past 0.47 s doesn't help.** At 5 mm a deadline of 0.35, 0.47 and 0.55 s catches 66, 67 and 67, and at 2 mm all catch 90.
8. **12 knots costs nothing measurable.** On the same schedule 24 knots catches the same, 90 and 67, with 50 against 48 on the pad at 2 mm
   and 36 against 37 at 5 mm. At its own measured delay, 24 knots has no time for any replan: `results/replan_timing.md`.
9. **The free first solve is the biggest assumption, and A and B both rest on it.** Charging A's one solve 45 ms, from a start 45 ms later,
   drops it from 68 to 15 at 2 mm and from 11 to 3 at 5 mm, with 82 and 83 tosses having no motion: the arm has about 40 ms of margin at
   0.10 s. B's first solve is A's, so it would do about the same (not run). A cold 24-knot first solve really takes a median of about 140 ms and a p95
   of about 320 ms on this laptop, so neither approach could run this way on it. The baseline can: its plan needs no measurements, so it can be computed
   before the toss.
10. **What's left for B is A's first solve.** All 10 of B's misses at 2 mm have no motion, as do 29 of its 33 at 5 mm. When the fit at 0.10 s
    puts the target out of reach, B never gets moving, and a retry 135 ms later is too late. Starting B from the baseline's plan, which
    needs no measurements, might address this and the assumption in finding 9 together. That would change what the spec calls B, and it hasn't been tried.
11. **The command stays continuous (B4).** Across all 1979 of B's motions, the commanded speed never exceeds 0.85 of rated and no step in commanded
    velocity exceeds what the plan's acceleration limit allows. Peak tracking error is no worse than A's: at 2 mm, a median of 45 mrad for both and at most
    70 mrad for B against 71 mrad for A. This depends on each replan starting from where the current plan will be when it takes over,
    which corrects section 8 of the spec.
12. **Re-runs repeat.** B's nominal cell run twice changed no outcome, no solve count and no adoption time. The one difference past the tolerances is the
    largest command step on one trial, by 0.0085. Details are in `planning/assumptions.md`.
13. **Some catches still rely on overspeed, as A's do.** 4 of B's 90 catches and 9 of its 91 on the fresh tosses go past 1.05x, against 3 of 68 and 9
    of 70 for A. B's worst is 1.09x, and A's 1.10x.

## What this doesn't show

- **The cell was picked after seeing A's and the baseline's results**, though not B's, and 2 mm is a good sensor. Trials 100 to 199 show the result
  doesn't depend on the tosses the cell was chosen from.
- **Solve times come from a laptop running a general-purpose solver**, not a real-time controller. Doubling the delay is the only check of that here.
- **No drag, no spin, and a known gravity.** The fit is exact for this ball, as `results/replan_timing.md` shows, which suits a one-shot fit as well as a recursive one.
- **Single catches only**, which departs from the proposal.

## Time per trial

A trial averages 8.7 s on one of 7 workers: 8.0 s for the baseline, 8.5 s for A, and 8.9 s for B, whose median of 6 solves adds about 0.6 s. Section 13.4's
1500 trials per approach then take about 90 minutes for all three, under the 2 hours `results/approach_a.md` allowed.
