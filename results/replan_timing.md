# What sets Approach B's constants

Generated by `HEADLESS=1 python replan_timing.py`, which takes several minutes. This is B6, and steps 2 and 3 of the plan in
`planning/approach-b-closed-loop-replanning.md`. The tables under each section are its output; the text here is the interpretation.
Solve times are one trial at a time on a laptop, with a general-purpose nonlinear solver, so they aren't what an embedded controller
would see. Seven parallel workers roughly double them, so the times recorded during sweeps can't set the delay.

None of this looks at whether a ball was caught, so the delay and the period were set without seeing B's result.

## What this sets

| Constant | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Replans | Warm-started from the current plan's tail, 12 knots | A warm 24-knot replan has a p95 of 184 to 227 ms. With one solver at a time, the first replan would start at about 0.29 to 0.33 s and take over at 0.47 to 0.56 s, so no replan makes the 0.47 s deadline. Warm 12-knot replans have a p95 of 39 to 42 ms |
| `TAU_COMPUTE` | 45 ms | That p95, rounded up to a multiple of the 5 ms control period, as section 9 asks |
| `REPLAN_PERIOD` | 45 ms, the same | One solver at a time. The next solve can't start until the last is done, because it has to start from the plan that one produced. The spec's 20 ms with this delay isn't something a real system could do |
| `COLD_DELAY_FACTOR` | 3 | A cold 12-knot solve has a p95 of 85 to 103 ms, 2.2 to 2.5 times a warm one. It only applies when B has no plan yet |

That allows at most 7 replans, taking over from 0.191 to 0.461 s. The honest description is **replanning at about 22 Hz with a 45 ms
delay, and a first solve that takes no time, as Approach A's does.**

## Findings

1. **The flight is ballistic, so recursive least squares is the right estimator (spec section 6.2).** With a perfect sensor the fit leaves
   2.1e-15 m, because the simulator's integrator keeps a parabola exactly. With 2 mm of sensor noise the residuals are 1.95 to 1.98 mm,
   as expected after fitting two parameters to about 56 samples. Their autocorrelations are all small and slightly negative, averaging
   -0.03, which is about what the fit itself causes. There's nothing here for a Kalman filter's process noise to model. An earlier run of
   this check gave 2.48 mm vertically, because it included samples after the ball had touched the hand.
2. **Warm starts are about 1.7 to 2.7 times faster than cold, and don't change the answer (section 7.2).** Warm and cold solutions fail on
   the same problems, and differ by at most 3.4 mrad at 12 knots and 4.5 mrad at 24. Both end the same distance from the target.
3. **The spec's 15 ms was optimistic by a factor of about 3,** at 12 knots rather than 24. That is what moves the design from 50 Hz with a
   20 ms delay to 22 Hz with a 45 ms delay.
4. **12 knots passes section 11.2's check.** Planned peak speed and the distance from the target are the same as at 24 knots, and the two
   differ by a median of 0.77 mrad, at most 5.78 mrad. It fails slightly more often, 13 of 75 solves against 10. Whether it costs catches
   is tested directly in `results/approach_b.md`, with 24 knots on the same schedule.
5. **Replans fail often, whatever the knots or the guess:** 13 of 75 at 50 ms between solves, and warm and cold fail together. That points at
   the problem rather than the solver: from where the arm is, in the time left, some targets can't be reached at 0.85 of rated speed. A
   failed replan keeps the current plan, so it costs one cycle.
6. **Every plan stops 5.2 mm from its target, always in the same direction.** That distance is the corner of `POSITION_TOL`'s +-3 mm box,
   (+3, +3, +3) mm: the planner's smoothness cost pushes the end as far as the constraint lets it, at 12 and at 24 knots, and for
   every approach. This has two consequences:
   - **`arm_arrival_error` isn't the tracker alone, as the trial record and earlier reports say.** On a noiseless catch the plan stops
     0.52 cm from the target, the real pinch point is 0.71 cm from where the plan stops, and these partly cancel to give the reported 0.55 cm.
     On six noisy tosses the tracking part is 0.65 to 0.82 cm, and the total 0.30 to 0.79 cm. No catch result changes, only what that
     number means.
   - **It limits what B can gain late.** B's fit reaches 0.59 cm by 0.15 s and 0.11 cm by 0.40 s, already smaller than these two
     errors. Tightening `POSITION_TOL` might help every approach, but it's shared by all three and would move their numbers, so it hasn't
     been tried.

## 1. Is the flight ballistic?

With a perfect sensor, the fit to one flight's 57 samples leaves at most 2.1e-15 m.
