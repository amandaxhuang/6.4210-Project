# Catch timing and tracking (week 2)

Generated by `HEADLESS=1 python catch_timing.py`, which takes roughly an hour and a half on 7 workers (the sections were
last run in separate batches, so that isn't a single timed run). Naming sections runs only those, for example
`python catch_timing.py tilt time_step`. The tables under each section are its output; the text above them is the
interpretation. Times are seconds after release unless noted. Catch motions are planned at 0.85 of rated speed. Everything but
sections 1 and 7, which don't involve a catch motion, was re-run at that limit; `results/approach_a.md` says why it was added.

## Findings

1. **The baseline and Approach A both catch a noiseless toss.** Both run end to end in Meshcat and headless, and hold
   the ball until the simulation ends, about 1 s later. The baseline aims at the toss's measured crossing of the catch
   plane, 1.4 cm from the planned one.
2. **The gripper's response has a 9 ms gap.** The pads can't reach ball width 20 to 29 ms after a WSG controller update:
   a command just before an update arrives at 20 ms, and one just after it at 29 ms (section 1). The nominal toss crosses
   the catch plane 25.4 ms after an update, inside that gap, so the pads can only get there 4.6 ms after the ball or
   about 6 ms before it.
3. **A3 passes at this report's force threshold, but 55 ms isn't the place to aim.** A perfect toss is caught at leads
   from 53 to 71 ms (section 2). The middle of that, 62 ms, is 7 ms from the spec's prediction, inside the 10 ms allowed,
   though it is a plateau rather than the peak A3 expects. 55 ms itself is only 2 ms from the late edge: at 52 ms the ball
   ends up on the floor. The early edge isn't physical (finding 5).
4. **The timing window is about 22 ms, not the spec's 7 ms, but most of it is a perch.** Perches count as catches under
   spec section 13.3, and every results table reports on-pad catches beside them.
   The pads can arrive anywhere from 15.4 ms before the ball to 6.6 ms after it. Only leads of 54 and 55 ms end with the
   ball's center on the pad along both axes: a 2 ms window that includes the spec's 55 ms, so the spec's arithmetic is right
   for a catch between the pads. From 56 to 71 ms the ball ends 1.3 to 2.5 cm along the fingers from the pad's center, above the pad's
   top edge, so the pads touch it below its equator. It holds for the full second and meets spec section 13.3's
   definition, but it is sitting on the pad edges rather than between the pads. **The default trigger's own noiseless
   catch is one of these**, at 1.47 cm. On noisy tosses, 7 of the 45 catches at 2 mm have the ball on the pad along both
   axes (section 5). Whether a perched ball would survive the arm moving hasn't been tested. Single catches don't need it
   to, but a demo that moves the ball afterward would.
5. **The window's early edge comes from the scoring, not the physics.** The spec asks for a non-zero grip force, and
   `GRIP_FORCE_MIN = 1 N` is this code's choice. Past 71 ms nothing changes physically: the fingers are still closing when
   the ball touches them, and they go on squeezing it with less and less force as the opening narrows. The ball is still up
   there at 89 ms and has fallen by 90 ms (sections 2 and 6). At 0.1 N the window would reach at least 80 ms, and its middle
   would be 11.5 ms from the prediction, which fails A3 as worded. Noisy catch rates barely depend on it: 45 to 49 of
   60 at 2 mm, and 10 to 11 at 5 mm. It stays at 1 N, the conservative choice.
6. **Sideways tolerance depends on when the fingers close.** Near the default trigger (56 ms) it is -2.0 to +2.0 cm
   along the closing axis and +-2.5 cm across the pads. Closing later, at 55 ms, widens the closing axis to +-3.0 cm,
   because the ball is already between the fingers, but the across-pad range becomes lopsided. Closing earlier narrows
   the closing axis, to -1.0 to +1.0 cm at 66 ms (section 3). Aiming 3.5 cm or more to one side across the pads has no motion.
   These are one axis at a time with perfect timing, on a
   0.5 cm grid, so real errors, which combine, will have less room.
7. **The ball comes in 36 degrees off the finger axis.** The gripper is 23.7 degrees from up, close to the 25 degree limit,
   and leans away from the ball's approach. So a timing error also moves the ball across the pads: it ends +0.76 cm
   across when +1.47 cm along the fingers. Finding 15 tests a tighter limit.
8. **Tracking: 0.55 cm at arrival, and the driver stays `position_only`** (section 4). On noisy targets the arrival
   error is 0.5 cm at the median and at most 0.97 cm (section 5).
   - The 2.03 cm peak is at the hand-off, before the catch motion has done anything: the arm hasn't settled from the
     brake.
   - Joint 6 sits at its torque limit from about 0.11 to 0.18 s, where the simulator clips it silently. By 0.40 s it has
     recovered to 5 mrad, so this doesn't reach the arrival.
   - The arrival error builds through the final deceleration: joint 6 goes from -10 mrad at 0.50 s to -28 mrad on arrival,
     while the plan is still decelerating at 20 rad/s^2. It then takes about 200 ms to decay.
   - Joint 6 reaches 0.96 of rated speed. The plan stays within 0.85, so this is tracking overshoot, and the paper should
     state it. Noisy tosses overshoot further, up to 1.11 at 0.10 s, about 0.16 s into the motion: `results/approach_a.md`.
   - The other drivers weren't tried, since they would change the toss. 0.55 cm is small against the 2 to 3 cm tolerances
     and the estimator's error, and the noiseless catch works with it.
9. **The lookup looks worth keeping, and 3 ms is a reasonable aim, but 60 trials can't prove either.** At 2 mm it catches
   45 of 60 against 39 for the fixed 55 ms lead, and at 5 mm 9 to 12 against 10 (section 5). Pad leads of 0 to 5 ms can't
   be separated; 12 ms is clearly too early at 2 mm. 3 ms is the middle of the plateau, and puts the noiseless catch about
   13 ms from the late edge and 9 ms from the early one. If only catches on the pad mattered, 0 ms would be the better aim:
   the noiseless catch then ends on the pad, 2 ms from the late edge, and at 2 mm it puts 11 of 60 on the pad against 7.
10. **A preview of the nominal cell, not a result.** With no toss noise, Approach A catches 17% of tosses at
   sigma_p = 5 mm with 72% contact, and 75% at 2 mm with 100% contact. On the pad alone, it is 0% and 12%. The planner found
   no motion in 13 of 60 at 5 mm and none at 2 mm. Spec section 14 predicted well below a coin flip at 5 mm, and 17% is at the
   edge of what section 15 says should move the nominal cell. Step 9's grid, with toss noise, is in `results/approach_a.md`.
11. **Toss noise works, and A4 passes.** The noise goes straight onto the ball's velocity 8 ms after release, once the ball
    has left the fingers and before the first observation. The spread in where and when the ball comes down is within 12%
    of the ballistic prediction, against the 15% allowed (section 7). Rows at the same kind of noise scale the same draws,
    so they aren't independent evidence. With a perfect sensor the fit recovers the perturbed flight, with a median
    predicted miss of 0.0 cm (section 8).
12. **At the spec's nominal cell, the baseline beats Approach A.** With sigma lateral and vertical both 0.02 m/s, the
    baseline catches 49 of 60 without looking, while Approach A catches 42 at sigma_p = 2 mm and 9 at 5 mm. With a perfect
    sensor it catches all 60 (section 8). So at these levels the one-shot fit's own error is larger than the toss noise it
    is there to correct: 2.87 cm and about 11 ms at 5 mm, against 1.13 cm and 3.8 ms from the toss. The baseline
    falls away as the toss gets noisier, to 29 of 60 at 0.04 m/s and 9 at 0.08 m/s. This bears on spec Q3 and on the
    proposal's "how much each approach gains from sensing": here the gain is negative for Approach A. The 100-trial grid in
    `results/approach_a.md` agrees, at 80 for the baseline against 72 and 16. Approach B hasn't been run.
13. **sigma lateral = 0.08 m/s doesn't defeat the arm, as the specs expected.** With a perfect sensor Approach A catches 40
    of 60, which is every toss it found a motion for. The other 20 (33%, past the spec's 5% threshold for a finding) have no
    motion, consistent with the correction envelope but not checked one by one. Approach B's prediction that its gap
    closes at 0.08 m/s can't be assumed, and a larger level may be needed to show it.
14. **0.15 s doesn't work, and between 0.10 s and 0.125 s neither is clearly better at this toss noise.** At sigma lateral and
    vertical 0.02 m/s, Approach A catches 42, 43 and 2 of 60 at 2 mm for 0.10, 0.125 and 0.15 s, and 9, 15 and 2 at 5 mm (section 9). The
    predicted miss and the timing error both fall by about half by 0.15 s, but by then 56 to 58 tosses have no motion. This is the
    failure the spec expected. At 0.165 s nothing moves: about half the tosses are predicted to come down too soon to reach, so
    `intercept` rejects them, and the baseline's motion they fall back to is out of reach from there too. The 100-trial grid in
    `results/approach_a.md` covers more toss noise. All of this charges no time for the solve, which the spec gives Approach A by
    construction. An earlier version of this finding said 0.15 s was better than 0.10 s; that was at the toss's 0.986 of rated speed, and
    relied on speeds the arm isn't rated for.
15. **The 25 degree tilt limit stays (spec Q5).** A tighter limit loses reach. At 15 degrees the earliest arrival is 25 ms too late, so the
    noiseless catch has no motion, and noisy tosses catch 5 of 60 against 42 at 25 degrees (section 10). 20 degrees has 13 ms to spare
    against 40 at 25 degrees, catches 29 with no motion for 18 tosses, and gains nothing that was measured: the ball still ends 1.6 cm
    along the fingers.
16. **A 1 ms plant time step is small enough (spec Q7).** Of 30 noisy trials, none changed outcome at 0.5 ms, 20 were
    caught at each step, and the ball ended a median of 0.15 cm from where it did at 1 ms, at most 0.28 cm (section 11).

## Still open

- **The nominal cell and window** (spec Q3): the data and a recommendation are in `results/approach_a.md`.
- **The latency sweep needs defining.** The trigger subtracts the latency it is configured with, so sweeping it leaves
  Approach A's fingers moving at the same time and changes nothing. To show an effect, set `assumed_latency` so the
  trigger assumes one latency while the plumbing applies another.
- Not run: any test of a perched ball under arm motion.
