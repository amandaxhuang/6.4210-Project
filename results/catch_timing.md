# Catch timing and tracking (week 2)

Generated by `HEADLESS=1 python catch_timing.py`, which takes about 40 minutes on 7 workers. The tables under each
section are its output; the text above them is the interpretation. Times are seconds after release unless noted.

## Findings

1. **The baseline and Approach A both catch a noiseless toss.** Both run end to end in Meshcat and headless, and hold
   the ball until the simulation ends, about 1 s later. The baseline aims at the toss's measured crossing of the catch
   plane, 1.4 cm from the planned one.
2. **The gripper's response has a 9 ms gap.** The pads can't reach ball width 20 to 29 ms after a WSG controller update:
   a command just before an update arrives at 20 ms, and one just after it at 29 ms (section 1). The nominal toss crosses
   the catch plane 25.4 ms after an update, inside that gap, so the pads can only get there 4.6 ms after the ball or
   about 6 ms before it.
3. **A3 passes at this report's force threshold, but 55 ms isn't the place to aim.** A perfect toss is caught at leads
   from 53 to 71 ms (section 2). The middle of that, 62 ms, is 7 ms from the spec's prediction, inside the 10 ms allowed,
   though it is a plateau rather than the peak A3 expects. 55 ms itself is only 2 ms from the late edge: at 52 ms the ball
   isn't held, and by 51 ms it ends up on the floor. The early edge isn't physical (finding 5).
4. **The timing window is about 22 ms, not the spec's 7 ms, but most of it is a perch. This needs a team decision.**
   The pads can arrive anywhere from 15.4 ms before the ball to 6.6 ms after it. Only leads of 53 to 55 ms end with the
   ball's center on the pad: a 3 ms window, 1 ms from the spec's prediction, so the spec's arithmetic is right for a catch
   between the pads. From 56 to 71 ms the ball ends 1.3 to 2.5 cm along the fingers from the pad's center, above the pad's
   top edge, so the pads touch it below its equator. It holds for the full second and meets spec section 13.3's
   definition, but it is sitting on the pad edges rather than between the pads. **The default trigger's own noiseless
   catch is one of these**, at 1.45 cm. On noisy tosses, 12 of the 44 catches at 2 mm have the ball on the pad (section
   5). Whether a perched ball would survive the arm moving hasn't been tested. Every trial now records `end_in_hand`,
   so either definition can be applied afterward.
5. **The window's early edge comes from the scoring, not the physics.** The spec asks for a non-zero grip force, and
   `GRIP_FORCE_MIN = 1 N` is this code's choice. Past 71 ms nothing changes physically: the fingers are still closing when
   the ball touches them, and they go on squeezing it with less and less force as the opening narrows. It stays in both
   fingers out to 87 ms and rolls off at 88 ms (section 6). At 0.1 N the window would reach at least 80 ms, and its middle
   would be 11.5 ms from the prediction, which fails A3 as worded. Noisy catch rates barely depend on it: 44 to 47 of
   60 at 2 mm, and 9 to 10 at 5 mm. It stays at 1 N, the conservative choice, until the team settles finding 4, which
   would make it moot if only catches on the pad count.
6. **Sideways tolerance depends on when the fingers close.** Near the default trigger (56 ms) it is -2.0 to +1.5 cm
   along the closing axis and +-2.5 cm across the pads. Closing later, at 55 ms, widens the closing axis to +-3.0 cm,
   because the ball is already between the fingers, but the across-pad range becomes lopsided. Closing earlier narrows
   the closing axis, to -1.0 to +0.5 cm at 66 ms (section 3). These are one axis at a time with perfect timing, on a
   0.5 cm grid, so real errors, which combine, will have less room.
7. **The ball comes in 36 degrees off the finger axis.** The gripper is 23.8 degrees from up, close to the 25 degree limit,
   and leans away from the ball's approach. So a timing error also moves the ball across the pads: it ends +0.74 cm
   across when +1.45 cm along the fingers. Whether a tighter limit is still reachable is spec Q5, not tested here.
8. **Tracking: 0.56 cm at arrival, and the driver stays `position_only`** (section 4). On noisy targets the arrival
   error is 0.6 cm at the median and at most 0.98 cm (section 5).
   - The 2.03 cm peak is at the hand-off, before the catch motion has done anything: the arm hasn't settled from the
     brake.
   - Joint 6 sits at its torque limit from about 0.11 to 0.16 s, where the simulator clips it silently. By 0.40 s it has
     recovered to 5 mrad, so this doesn't reach the arrival.
   - The arrival error builds through the final deceleration: joint 6 goes from -13 mrad at 0.50 s to -28 mrad on arrival,
     while the plan is still decelerating at 17 rad/s^2. It then takes about 200 ms to decay.
   - Joint 2 reaches 1.02 x its velocity limit. The plan stays within 0.986 x, so this is tracking overshoot, and the paper
     should state it.
   - The other drivers weren't tried, since they would change the toss. 0.56 cm is small against the 2 to 3 cm tolerances
     and the estimator's error, and the noiseless catch works with it.
9. **The lookup looks worth keeping, and 3 ms is a reasonable aim, but 60 trials can't prove either.** At 2 mm it catches
   44 of 60 against 37 for the fixed 55 ms lead, and at 5 mm 9 to 12 against 9 (section 5). Pad leads of 0 to 5 ms can't
   be separated; 12 ms is clearly too early at 2 mm. 3 ms is the middle of the plateau, and puts the noiseless catch about
   13 ms from the late edge and 9 ms from the early one. If the team rejects perches, 0 ms is the better aim: the noiseless
   catch then ends on the pad, 2 ms from the late edge.
10. **A preview of the nominal cell, not a result.** With no toss noise, Approach A catches 15% of tosses at
   sigma_p = 5 mm with 92% contact, and 73% at 2 mm with 100% contact. On the pad alone, it is 7% and 20%. The planner found
   no motion in 1 of 60 at 5 mm and none at 2 mm. Spec section 14 predicted well below a coin flip at 5 mm, and 15% is at the
   edge of what section 15 says should move the nominal cell. Step 9 decides this.
11. **Toss noise works, and A4 passes.** The noise goes straight onto the ball's velocity 8 ms after release, once the ball
    has left the fingers and before the first observation. The spread in where and when the ball comes down is within 12%
    of the ballistic prediction, against the 15% allowed (section 7). Rows at the same kind of noise scale the same draws,
    so they aren't independent evidence. With a perfect sensor the fit recovers the perturbed flight, with a median
    predicted miss of 0.0 cm (section 8).
12. **At the spec's nominal cell, the baseline beats Approach A.** With sigma lateral and vertical both 0.02 m/s, the
    baseline catches 49 of 60 without looking, while Approach A catches 42 at sigma_p = 2 mm and 9 at 5 mm. With a perfect
    sensor it catches all 60 (section 8). So at these levels the one-shot fit's own error is larger than the toss noise it
    is there to correct: 2.87 cm and about 11 ms at 5 mm, against 1.13 cm and 3.8 ms from the toss. The baseline
    falls away as the toss gets noisier, to 29 of 60 at 0.04 m/s and 9 at 0.08 m/s. This bears on spec Q3 and on the
    proposal's "how much each approach gains from sensing": here the gain is negative for Approach A. These are 60-trial
    previews without the step 9 harness, and Approach B hasn't been run.
13. **sigma lateral = 0.08 m/s doesn't defeat the arm, as the specs expected.** With a perfect sensor Approach A catches 51
    of 60, which is every toss it found a motion for. The other 9 (15%, past the spec's 5% threshold for a finding) have no
    motion, consistent with the correction envelope but not checked one by one. Approach B's prediction that its gap
    closes at 0.08 m/s can't be assumed, and a larger level may be needed to show it.

## Still open

- **What counts as a catch** (findings 4 and 5): whether a perched ball counts, and what grip force is non-zero.
- **The nominal cell** (spec Q3): finding 12 adds a reason to revisit it, since sigma_p = 5 mm also leaves Approach A well
  behind the baseline.
- **The latency sweep needs defining.** The trigger subtracts the latency it is configured with, so sweeping it leaves
  Approach A's fingers moving at the same time and changes nothing. To show an effect, the trigger has to assume one
  latency while the plumbing applies another.
- **Per-trial time:** about 6 s per trial on each of 7 workers, so 4500 trials would take about an hour before any extra
  solves for Approach B. This is a rough figure from these sweeps, to be measured properly in step 9.
- Not run yet: `T_WINDOW` sensitivity (section 14), the 0.5 ms time step (Q7), and any test of a perched ball under arm
  motion.
