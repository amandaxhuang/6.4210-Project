# Catch feasibility (week 2)

Generated by `HEADLESS=1 python feasibility.py` (about 75 s). Tables below are its output; this
header is the interpretation. Times are seconds after release unless noted.

## Findings

1. **The current toss (65°) cannot be caught in time.** The ball reaches the catch point
   0.338 s after release; the arm gets there and stops 0.60–0.66 s after release in every case,
   including with unlimited acceleration. A separate check from 40 random IK starts, ignoring
   the ball, still needs at least 0.56 s of motion after the brake. The elbow, joint 4,
   is the bottleneck: reaching out from 0.67 m to 0.86 m takes about 0.66 rad of joint 4 at
   no more than 1.18 rad/s.
2. **Toss more steeply instead.** At the same 1.5 m/s the ball comes down closer to where the
   hand stopped. 75° is borderline (25 ms to spare after the brake); 80° leaves 142 ms
   and 85° leaves 159 ms. The proposal already calls for a toss that is "mostly upward".
3. **The catch relies on the brake's high accelerations.** Under the swing's acceleration
   (3.6 v_max per second) no angle makes it in time: even straight up arrives 1 ms late.
   With the brake's (18 v_max per second, 24–42 rad/s²) it works from 75° up. The model has
   no acceleration limits and the simulator does not enforce any, so this has to be stated
   as an assumption in the paper, and the catch motion's tracking checked in simulation.
4. **Skipping the brake helps little.** Starting the catch straight from the release state
   saves 52 ms at 75° but only 0–7 ms at 80–90°, and enlarges the envelope 1–3 cm at 80°. It
   still needs the brake's accelerations: at the swing's, the hand cannot fall away from the
   ball faster than gravity and the ball never leaves. Keeping toss.py's brake-to-stop is the simpler choice.
5. **Long tosses are the binding direction.** The envelope toward +x (ball lands farther from
   the robot) is small: 0.4–0.7 cm at 75°, 2–5 cm at 80°, 4–10 cm at 85° for most of the
   flight (after the brake, for t_c up to 0.25 s). -x (toward the robot) and ±y are 10–40 cm
   early on. Toss variability should be sized against +x, or the nominal catch moved inward.
6. **Position-only sensing gives the A vs B comparison room; velocity readings mostly erase
   it.** With σ_p = 5 mm, a prediction from the first 100 ms is off by 1.5 cm (1σ per axis),
   about the gripper's ±1.6 cm tolerance. At 80° the arm can still move the catch 2+ cm
   toward +x until about 0.25 s, by which time the refit is good to 0.3 cm, so continual
   replanning has real work to do. With σ_v = 0.05 m/s readings, the 100 ms prediction is
   already good to 0.5 cm and there is little left for replanning to fix.
7. **Gripper: 20–28 ms to close to the ball's width.** The fingers start moving 3 ms after the
   command; the rest depends on where the command lands in the WSG controller's 50 ms update.
   The 8 ms spread is 1.6 cm of ball travel at 2 m/s, close to the 2 cm pad height, so the
   close trigger should account for it. Force limit (20–80 N) makes no difference with nothing
   in the fingers. The real WSG 50 is rated at around 420 mm/s, so the simulated gripper
   (1.4–2.1 m/s opening rate) is likely several times faster than hardware.

## Suggested decisions

- Set `TOSS_ANGLE` to 80° (or 85° for more +x margin) and rerun `toss.py` to confirm the
  release still matches the plan.
- Keep brake-to-stop as the hand-off.
- Make position-only the headline observation mode, or raise σ_v, and say why in the paper.
- Close-trigger lead: about 25 ms of gripper time plus the decision-to-action delay.

## How it was computed, and caveats

- Each motion is a trajectory optimization over 24 knots (velocity piecewise linear), within
  90% of the velocity limits (`VELOCITY_MARGIN`, as in toss.py), the joint position limits,
  and the stated acceleration limit, ending at rest with the pinch point within 3 mm of the
  target and the gripper within 25° of up. The final pose is chosen by the optimizer, not fixed
  to `catch_pose`'s answer.
- The pinch point is kept at or below the ball's planned height until the catch, so the ball
  leaves the hand and later comes down into it. This is a height-only check.
- Minimum times come from bisection; a solver failure is not proof that no motion exists, so
  each minimum time is an upper bound and each envelope a lower bound.
- The envelope fixes the catch time and moves the target horizontally only. Errors in the
  vertical release speed also shift the arrival time, which this does not cover.
- The nominal motion for the envelope is the smoothest one arriving exactly with the ball.
- Prediction error is analytic least squares on (p0, v0) per horizontal axis, observations at
  100 Hz from release to t_c. "inf" means one sample, too few to fit a velocity.
- The gripper test closes on nothing. Closing on an off-center ball will touch one pad sooner.
